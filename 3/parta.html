Section 01

Visualizing Training and Sampling

Unlike traditional diffusion models, which typically work backward from noise toward a clean image, flow matching learns a continuous path that transforms random noise at t = 0 into a clean image at t = 1. In optimal transport (OT) flow matching, this path is a straight-line interpolation between noise and the original image. The model learns to predict the direction and speed of movement along this path, known as the velocity, so it can gradually transform noise into an image.

xt = (1 - t) ε + t x1,   ε ~ N(0, I),   vgt = x1 - ε

1.1 Constructing Noisy Training Inputs

To get a better feel for how this process works, I experimented with my own test image. Using a single sample of Gaussian noise, I generated intermediate states at t = 0.25, 0.50, and 0.75. Comparing these snapshots helped me visualize how the image evolves along its trajectory, with the image content becoming clearer and the noise fading as t approaches 1.

1.5 Iterative Denoising with Euler Integration

Because real probability trajectories curve in high dimensions, extrapolating in a single giant jump fails at high noise levels. Instead, we take small numerical Euler steps (h = 0.02), recomputing the local velocity along the trajectory. Starting at t = 0.50, it takes 25 steps to reach t = 1.00.

Comparison on the same t = 0.50 input: Original vs Gaussian Blur vs One-step vs Euler Denoising.

What I learned

Classical filtering acts as a low-pass filter: it suppresses noise by sacrificing fine details. One-step flow extrapolation is a huge leap forward because it hallucinate coherent visual semantics, but it struggles when curvature is pronounced. Euler sampling solves this by treating generation as following an empirical vector field step-by-step.

Section 02

Conditional Generation and Guidance

Can we generate pictures from pure static noise (t = 0.0)? Yes, by running Euler sampling across 50 steps. However, standard sampling often produces washed-out or ambiguous faces. Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) boosts prompt adherence by combining conditional and unconditional predictions.

vcfg = vc + w (vc - vu)

2.1 Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) Comparisons

Evaluating the exact same 5 initial random noise seeds across unguided sampling (w = 0) and guided sampling (w = 7.0) with the prompt "a high quality photo" demonstrates how CFG carves crisp contrast, distinct features, and vivid lighting out of blurry averages.

The Trade-off with Guidance

CFG is like turning up the saturation knob on the semantic concept itself. By steering away from the empty-string prediction, the vector field is forced into sharper, higher-density likelihood modes. The output loses diversity across strange compositions, but gains dramatic fidelity and sharp edges.

Section 03

Creative Applications

Once we control the vector field through conditional Euler sampling, we can bend the trajectory to perform image-to-image editing, sketch-to-photo projection, 180° visual anagrams, and multiresolution hybrid images.

3.1 Image-to-Image Editing (SDEdit)

By adding noise corresponding to a starting time tstart ∈ [0.02, 0.04, 0.06, 0.10, 0.14, 0.25, 0.50] and denoising back to t = 1.0, we project the image back onto the manifold of natural images. Lower tstart allows wild hallucinations, while higher tstart preserves fine details.

Final Takeaway

Visual anagrams and hybrid images reveal how flexible vector fields really are. Because the velocity predictions at each step are just vector arithmetic in pixel space, we can rotate them, filter their frequency bands, or average them between prompts. The flow model happily walks down these composited paths, creating images that satisfy contradictory human interpretations simultaneously.
